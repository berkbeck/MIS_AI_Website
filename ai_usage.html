Correct AI Usage

Artificial intelligence can help college students like use understand difficult concepts by explaining them in a more easy to read way. Students can use AI to brainstorm ideas, create practice questions, and review grammar. However, should be careful and make sure to check facts, follow rules set by instructors, and state when AI is used and what it is used for.

ChatGPT's evaluation: We agree with this reasoning because AI can support learning without replacing a students effort. Students like us should verify the information given to us by AI and make sure it follows course policies. AI can also state false or inaccurate information, so students should compare these statements with reliable sources. Protection personal information is another very important part of responsible AI use.

Benefits of AI

  1. Explains challenging concepts and provides practice questions
  2. Helps students brainstorm ideas and improve their writing

Who benefits from AI?

  1. Student who use AI strictly for understanding
  2. Teachers who use AI to support learning
  3. Organizations responsibly using AI

How AI Tools Can Be Used

  • Reviewing concepts and creating study questions
  • Checking grammar and organizing our ideas

Incorrect AI Usage

Submitting AI work as your own
Presenting AI generated work as your own when that violates your instructors set rules.
Using false or unverified information
Relying on invented information or inaccurate information without checking reliable sources.
Avoiding the learning process
Copying the answers that AI generates for you without understanding the material or even completing the required work.

In our opinion, AI is helpful when students use it with honesty and responsibility. Students should try and find a general understanding of their assignments rather than relying completely on AI to do all of the thinking for them. They should always check the important facts and information along with following their instructions policies laid out in their syllabus. Misusing AI can weaken learning and damage trust.
